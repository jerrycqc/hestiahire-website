Content marketing kept showing up on the roadmap and kept getting bumped, because there was always something more urgent for a four-person team to do.

**Why it never happened organically**

We asked the founder to estimate how long a single blog post actually took him to write. His answer: most of a day, by the time he context-switched into writing mode, drafted, and edited. At that cost, content marketing was never going to survive contact with a growing product roadmap.

**Removing the founder as the bottleneck, not the source**

The fix wasn't asking the founder to write less carefully, it was removing him from the first draft entirely. The marketing assistant sources real material from customer calls and support tickets, drafts a post, and the founder's only remaining job is a 30-minute technical accuracy review before it goes out.

**Results**

The company went from three posts a year to a real weekly cadence within a month of onboarding. Organic search traffic to the blog was up roughly 5x within two quarters, and the founder now spends less total time on content than he did writing three posts a year, while publishing fifty times as often.
