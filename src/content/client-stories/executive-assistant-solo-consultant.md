Independent consultants often hit the same wall: the business grows exactly as fast as the founder can personally handle both the client work and everything around it. She'd hit that wall hard.

**Where the 12 hours were actually going**

We had her track her own time for a week before matching her with anyone. Scheduling alone (finding times, sending confirmations, handling reschedules) was eating close to five hours. Inbox triage, invoicing follow-ups, and travel logistics made up the rest. None of it required her specific expertise, but all of it required her attention.

**Starting small, on purpose**

Rather than overcommitting to a full-time hire she wasn't sure she needed, she started on the part-time plan at 20 hours a week. Her assistant took over calendar management first, then inbox triage once trust was established, then proposals and invoicing by week four.

**What changed**

With scheduling and admin off her plate, she started saying yes to discovery calls she'd been declining. Three new clients signed in the following quarter, and she moved to the full-time plan once the added client load justified it, on her own timeline, not a forced upsell.
