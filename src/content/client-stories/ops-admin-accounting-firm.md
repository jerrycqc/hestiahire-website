Twelve people and three partners should have meant plenty of admin capacity. Instead, admin was everyone's second priority, which meant it was effectively nobody's job.

**The cost of no single owner**

We audited a month of onboarding files before proposing anything. The pattern was consistent: partners each had their own version of an intake checklist, occasionally skipped steps under time pressure, and follow-up emails to new clients or vendors sometimes went two or three days without a response because no one partner felt responsible for chasing it.

**One process, one owner**

Rather than assigning the new admin work along the same fragmented lines, the operations assistant built a single onboarding checklist covering document collection, compliance verification, and system setup, and became the one point of contact clients and vendors dealt with during intake. Partners got a weekly status digest instead of having to track it themselves.

**Results**

Onboarding time dropped from an average of 14 days to 3. Partners collectively got back roughly 6 hours a week they'd been spending on admin follow-up, time now going toward billable client work instead.
